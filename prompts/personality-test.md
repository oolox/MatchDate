**The Compass Inventory**

For each question, choose one answer. There are no right or wrong choices. Go with your first impulse.

---

**1. A friend calls you, upset about a personal failure. They don't want advice—they just need to vent. Do you:**

- **A)** Listen quietly, offer warmth, and reassure them they're not alone.
- **B)** After listening, gently help them see what they could learn from it.
- **C)** Feel a little restless—you want to fix the problem, not just sit in the feeling.
- **D)** Stay with them as long as it takes, even if it drains your own energy.

---

**2. You're planning a weekend trip. Which sounds most appealing?**

- **A)** A quiet cabin in the woods with no phone service. Books, fires, stillness.
- **B)** A bustling city you've never visited. New food, new streets, new people.
- **C)** A familiar beach town you've loved since childhood. Rituals, memories, comfort.
- **D)** A loose itinerary with room for spontaneous detours. You'll decide day by day.

---

**3. You're assigned a group project at work. The leader proposes a plan that you think is inefficient. You:**

- **A)** Voice your concern clearly, but follow the plan if overruled. Harmony matters.
- **B)** Respect the leader's role, but work quietly on your own improvement to the process.
- **C)** Advocate hard for your approach—the project's success is worth the friction.
- **D)** Step back and let the leader lead, even if it fails. They'll learn.

---

**4. At a party, you notice someone standing alone, looking uncomfortable. You:**

- **A)** Go introduce yourself. Draw them into conversation. Everyone deserves to be included.
- **B)** Notice them, but assume they might prefer their own space. You don't intrude.
- **C)** Send the host over—they're better at this than you are.
- **D)** Feel a tug of empathy, but you're in the middle of a great conversation. You'll check on them later.

---

**5. A controversial local issue is dividing your neighborhood. You:**

- **A)** Research the facts, then attend a town hall to advocate for what seems most fair for everyone.
- **B)** Talk to your neighbors one-on-one. Listen more than you speak. Look for common ground.
- **C)** Stay out of it. Controversy drains you, and you trust the system to sort itself out.
- **D)** Choose a side and show up. Passivity feels like complicity.

---

**6. You receive an unexpected inheritance—enough to change your life, but not enough to retire. You:**

- **A)** Invest it conservatively. Security and peace of mind matter more than risk.
- **B)** Use it to start or fund something you care about—a project, a cause, a small business.
- **C)** Spend a portion on something you've always wanted, and save the rest.
- **D)** Give a significant portion to family or community. Money is a tool for connection.

---

**7. Your morning routine is interrupted by something urgent but unplanned. You feel:**

- **A)** Irritated and unmoored. Routines are how you function.
- **B)** Flexible. You adapt, enjoy the novelty, and pick up the routine tomorrow.
- **C)** Stressed, but you hide it well. You're polite while internally recalibrating.
- **D)** Open. The interruption might be more interesting than your routine anyway.

---

**8. A close friend achieves something you've been working toward for years. What stirs first?**

- **A)** Genuine pride for them, followed by a quiet, private pang of envy you'll process alone.
- **B)** Immediate celebration. Their win is a win for your circle.
- **C)** Motivation—if they can do it, you can do it. You double your effort.
- **D)** A sense of distance. You're happy for them, but you feel the gap between your paths.

---

**9. When you make an important decision, what do you lean on most?**

- **A)** Logic, data, and the advice of people you trust who've been there.
- **B)** Your gut. You've learned to trust your instincts.
- **C)** What feels most aligned with your values, even if it's inefficient or impractical.
- **D)** What will cause the least harm to the people you care about.

---

**10. A stranger in line drops an armful of groceries. You're next. The cashier is waiting. You:**

- **A)** Immediately bend down to help, unconcerned about the delay.
- **B)** Hesitate a split second—you're in a rush—but then help.
- **C)** Offer a quick word of sympathy, but stay in line. Someone else will help.
- **D)** Help while making a joke to ease the stranger's embarrassment.
