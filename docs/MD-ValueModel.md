# Schwartz Value Model

Reference for the ten basic values, higher-order dimensions, and interpersonal compatibility in romantic relationships.

---

## The 10 Basic Values

| Value | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Self-Direction** | Independent thought and action, choosing, creating, and exploring. |
| **Stimulation** | Excitement, novelty, and challenge in life. |
| **Hedonism** | Pleasure and sensuous gratification for oneself. |
| **Achievement** | Personal success through demonstrating competence based on social standards. |
| **Power** | Social status, prestige, and control or dominance over people and resources. |
| **Security** | Safety, harmony, and stability of society, relationships, and the self. |
| **Conformity** | Restraint of actions and impulses that might upset or harm others and violate social norms. |
| **Tradition** | Respect, commitment, and acceptance of the customs and ideas traditional to one's culture or religion. |
| **Benevolence** | Preserving and enhancing the welfare of people with whom one has frequent personal contact. |
| **Universalism** | Understanding, appreciation, tolerance, and protection for the welfare of all people and nature. |

---

## Four Higher-Order Dimensions

The ten values organize into a circular continuum mapped across two major bipolar dimensions, grouped into four broader higher-order categories:

| Dimension | Values | Emphasis |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| **Openness to Change** | Self-Direction, Stimulation (and partly Hedonism) | Independent action and novelty |
| **Conservation** | Security, Conformity, Tradition | Order, self-restriction, and stability |
| **Self-Transcendence** | Benevolence, Universalism | Acceptance and concern for the welfare of others |
| **Self-Enhancement** | Power, Achievement (and partly Hedonism) | Social status and personal success |

---

## Interpersonal Compatibility

Interpersonal compatibility in the Schwartz model is determined by how closely aligned two people's values are on the circle. Because values reflect personal priorities and motivations, individuals whose dominant values sit next to each other experience natural synergy, while those with opposing dominant values often face psychological and behavioral friction.

### High Compatibility: Adjacent Values

People are highly compatible when their core values sit next to each other on the circle because their behaviors share similar underlying goals.

**The Nurturers (Benevolence & Universalism)**

Both focus on helping others. A Benevolent person focuses on family and friends, while a Universalist focuses on broader social justice and the environment. They easily agree on charity, community, and empathy.

**The Power Couple (Achievement & Power)**

Both prioritize success. One focuses on demonstrating competence, while the other focuses on status and control. They tend to respect each other's ambition, career drive, and desire for social standing.

**The Safe Harbors (Conformity & Security)**

Both deeply value stability, predictability, and rules. They find harmony in creating a highly structured, safe, and traditional home life without unexpected disruptions.

### Low Compatibility: Opposite Values

Friction occurs when one person's dominant value sits directly across the circle from the other's, creating a clash of fundamental motivations.

| Value clash | Motivation conflict | Real-world relationship friction |
|-------------|---------------------|----------------------------------|
| Benevolence vs. Power | Caring for others vs. dominating others | One partner prioritizes selflessness and helping loved ones, while the other prioritizes personal status, control, and material resources. |
| Universalism vs. Achievement | Social justice/nature vs. personal success | One person focuses on collective well-being and equality, while the other focuses strictly on individual advancement, competition, and social standards. |
| Self-Direction vs. Conformity | Independence vs. obedience | One partner needs absolute freedom to explore new ideas and break rules, while the other demands adherence to social expectations and rules. |
| Stimulation vs. Tradition | Novelty/excitement vs. preservation of customs | One person constantly craves change, risk, and new experiences, while the other finds comfort in routine, heritage, and time-honored rituals. |

### The Role of Hedonism

Hedonism (seeking pleasure and self-gratification) sits on the border between Openness to Change and Self-Enhancement.

- **Compatible with:** People high in Stimulation (both love a thrilling, fun lifestyle).
- **Conflicts with:** People high in Conformity or Tradition (who prioritize self-restraint and duty over immediate personal pleasure).

## Romantic Relationships

In a romantic relationship, the Schwartz Value Circle acts as a roadmap for daily harmony, lifestyle choices, and conflict resolution. When couples share adjacent values, they experience natural synergy. When their core values are opposites, it requires conscious compromise to avoid resentment.

### High-Friction Romances (Opposite Values)

#### 1. The Rebel & The Rule-Follower (Self-Direction vs. Conformity/Tradition)

- **The dynamic:** One partner craves independence, spontaneity, and unconventional choices. The other finds comfort in family expectations, social norms, and established routines.
- **Where they clash:** Deciding how to spend holidays (visiting extended family vs. a spontaneous backpacking trip), parenting styles (strict boundaries vs. total creative freedom), and lifestyle choices.
- **The compromise:** The Conformist partner must give the Self-Directed partner space for solo exploration, while the Self-Directed partner must respect and participate in key family traditions.

#### 2. The Thrill-Seeker & The Homebody (Stimulation vs. Security)

- **The dynamic:** One partner lives for novelty, risk, and constant change. The other deeply values safety, stability, financial predictability, and a peaceful environment.
- **Where they clash:** Financial decisions (investing in a risky startup vs. a safe savings account), moving houses, or weekend plans (skydiving vs. a quiet night at home).
- **The compromise:** Budgeting separate "risk money" and "safety savings," and alternating weekend activities so both needs are met.

#### 3. The Altruist & The Alpha (Benevolence/Universalism vs. Power/Achievement)

- **The dynamic:** One partner focuses on helping others, equality, and emotional connection. The other is driven by status, wealth, competition, and personal advancement.
- **Where they clash:** Time management (working 80 hours a week to get a promotion vs. coming home early for family time) and resource allocation (donating to charity vs. buying a luxury car).
- **The compromise:** Framing the career advancement as a tool that ultimately protects and provides for the family's well-being.

### Natural Matches (Adjacent Values)

#### 1. The Cozy Traditionalists (Security + Conformity + Tradition)

- **Why it works:** These couples build incredibly stable, loyal, and predictable lives. They usually agree easily on major milestones, family roles, and financial planning.
- **The risk:** The relationship can become stagnant or overly resistant to necessary life changes.

#### 2. The Power Couple (Achievement + Power)

- **Why it works:** Both partners understand and respect the other's ambition, late nights at the office, and desire for material success. They push each other to excel.
- **The risk:** They may treat the relationship like a business partnership, neglecting emotional intimacy or competing with each other.

#### 3. The Free Spirits (Self-Direction + Stimulation + Hedonism)

- **Why it works:** Life is an adventure. They travel, try new things, prioritize pleasure, and respect each other's need for personal freedom and space.
- **The risk:** A lack of grounding can lead to financial instability or difficulty committing to long-term responsibilities (like buying a home or raising children).
